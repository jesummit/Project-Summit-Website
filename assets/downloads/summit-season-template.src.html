The Season Planner
& fueling sheet

Structure your year with training load — the way the Gran Diagonal was built.

This is the framework behind the 999 km, six-day crossing of Portugal: how to lay a season out as a system — a target, a ramp, a load per week, a taper — and how to fuel the big efforts. Two worksheets follow. Fill them in for your own A-event.

The five rules that make it work

  1. 01Pick the A-day first, then count backwards. A rough template: ~12–16 weeks base, 6–10 build, 2–3 peak/taper. Everything else serves that date.
  2. 02Grow fitness slowly. A rise in CTL of roughly 4–6 TSS/week is sustainable for most trained amateurs; 8+ works for a short block but rarely holds. Ramp too fast and you don't pay day one — you pay in week three.
  3. 03Bank a recovery week every 3rd–4th week. Cut volume 30–40%, keep a little intensity. Fitness barely dips, fatigue clears, and the adaptation you already earned finally lands.
  4. 04Most weeks sit just above your 6-week average. That's the nudge that moves CTL up. Not every week a hero week — that's how ramps break.
  5. 05Taper into form. For the A-event, drop volume while keeping a little intensity so fatigue falls faster than fitness. Aim to arrive at a TSB of about +5 to +25: rested, not detrained.

Fuel the big days

Six days across Portugal burned ~25,400 kcal on the bike — about 4,200 a day. You never replace that. The job is to minimise the deficit so tomorrow's engine still starts. Here's what those days actually cost.

On the bike

  • Carbohydrate scaled to stage length, not appetite.
  • Long mountain days: top of what you absorb per hour; shorter days a notch below.
  • Carbs are the lever — at 6–9 h/day, fat can't cover the climb spikes.
  • Drink to thirst plus electrolytes; heat multiplies the loss.

The moment you stop

  • Carbohydrate + protein immediately — the window is real when you ride again in 16 h.
  • Then a proper meal, then keep grazing to bedtime.
  • Every gram in before sleep is fatigue you don't carry into tomorrow.

Read the drift

When heart rate at a given power creeps up across days, it's usually under-fueling before it's lost fitness. The fix is food, not heroics. Steady day-to-day HR at the same effort is the sign the load and the fueling are matched.

General guidance from one rider's data, not medical or individualised nutrition advice. Dial in your own numbers in training, never on the day.
