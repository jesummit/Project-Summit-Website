# VOC Research — Cyclists, Recovery & Adaptive Plans

**Date:** 2026-08-03 · **Mode:** Digital watering holes (proxy sources — Summit has no first-party reviews yet)

## Method & Sample

| Source | What was pulled | n |
|---|---|---|
| App Store customer reviews (US RSS, most recent) | WHOOP, TrainingPeaks, TrainerRoad, Athletica, Xert EBC | 315 reviews |
| TrainerRoad community forum (public Discourse search) | 4 queries on HRV / sleep score / recovery-driven training | 54 posts |

Analysis focused on the 1–3★ reviews mentioning recovery, sleep, HRV, adaptation or planning (23), the 4★ reviews (competitive intel: what is praised *and* what is missing, 6), and the forum posts.

**Sample bias — read before quoting any of this:**
- App Store reviews skew to problems and to whatever shipped most recently. **TrainerRoad's recent AI overhaul dominates its negative reviews**; that is a recency artifact, not a permanent market state.
- The TrainerRoad forum skews technical, data-literate and skeptical — more so than a mainstream App Store buyer.
- Nothing here is first-party. These are *proxy* sources: competitors' customers describing the problem space. Replace with Summit's own reviews as they arrive.
- No source is Spanish or Catalan. All language captured is English.

## Theme 1 — Distrust of "AI" in training apps · **Confidence: High**

Appears across three independent platforms (TrainerRoad reviews, WHOOP reviews, forum). Intensity: very high — this is the angriest language in the whole corpus.

- "The latest overhaul of the app is heavily AI-focused, and it's simply not ready for prime time… the current struggles are eerily familiar to the long wait for Adaptive Training to properly account for outdoor rides." — TrainerRoad, 2★
- "Long time user AI update was a HUGE miss. Companies really want their AI models to take over a this is a great example of half baked software." — TrainerRoad, 1★
- "This new model is suggesting workouts that make no sense from a scheduling perspective (e.g. suggesting I do a 9.6 rated 60 minute anaerobic work…)" — TrainerRoad, 2★
- "…what I've been doing that day and how I feel and not what AI thinks I need to do." — TrainerRoad, 2★
- "The AI assistant keeps pinging you and incorrectly reads its own data. It consistently contradicts itself." — WHOOP, 1★
- "The AI brain is the size of an acorn that's been flattened by a 18 wheeler truck!" — WHOOP, 2★
- "…the claim is combining Sleep data HRV data Optimized Training Stress to feed AI/ML algorithms and predict: readiness to train today… For someone in touch with their feelings, I'm skeptical." — TR forum

**Implication:** Summit's anti-AI framing is not a stylistic quirk, it is a wedge with live demand. The market is actively punishing AI-forward positioning in this exact category, right now. "Deterministic", "traceable", "not a black box" answer a complaint people are making unprompted.

## Theme 2 — Recovery scores that contradict how you feel destroy trust · **Confidence: High**

Three independent sources, high emotional intensity. This is the failure mode that makes people quit a recovery product.

- "the sleep metrics can just ruin your day even if you're feeling fine." — WHOOP, 1★
- "my recovery scores bear no resemblance to how I feel or perform. As a result I have decided to ditch whoop. I have lost all confidence in the system." — TR forum
- "Garmin gave me a sleep score of 84 last night despite waking up at least 4 times for the baby. My body battery showed 79 this morning but I don't really feel that good, so will take it easy despite what my watch says." — TR forum
- "Somehow the activity detection and sleep tracking has gotten worse… Like the idea, product is just not there yet." — WHOOP, 2★

**Implication:** Accuracy is table stakes, but *explainability* is the actual defence. A score people can interrogate ("what kind of night you had, and what explains it") survives a mismatch; a bare number does not. Summit's sleep breakdown is the right answer to this — but the copy currently sells the score, not the why.

## Theme 3 — Plans that don't survive real life · **Confidence: High**

Independent across TrainingPeaks and TrainerRoad, plus forum. Consistent, concrete, unprompted.

- "Wanted to switch my workout plan around for different days within the same week… It will allow you to work ahead in the week, but only if you execute the workout on rest day. If there is a different planned workout on the day you swap, it will show you as having missed one of the two workouts, even if you eventually accomplish both." — TrainingPeaks, 2★
- "There's also no flexibility at all… I'm in a group training for a marathon but not always follow every run as planed… the app won't allow me to do that." — TrainingPeaks, 2★
- "Workouts listed as optional, if not completed, get logged as red as missed workouts in Compliance." — TrainingPeaks, 2★
- "Doesn't adapt to you. The ai supposedly takes prior rides into account but it just starts you off with a PL 1 workout as if you've done 0 work before." — TrainerRoad, 1★

**Implication:** Direct validation of plan cascading. Note the emotional charge is about *being judged* — "logged as red", "shown as missed". The pain isn't only a broken schedule, it's the app scolding you for having a life. Copy that says "nothing breaks" is aimed correctly; consider saying explicitly that Summit doesn't mark you down.

## Theme 4 — Score fatigue and "listen to your body" · **Confidence: Medium**

Four independent posters, but all on one platform (TR forum), so medium. **This one cuts against Summit's pitch and deserves attention rather than burial.**

- "I had to get away from everything being scored or graded. I don't need a watch to…" — TR forum
- "Sleep. Rest. Eat. Listen to your body. Look at data afterwards. **The data should be descriptive, not prescriptive.**" — TR forum
- "I would never use it in place of 'feel'. Totally agree on that." — TR forum
- "But first and foremost I listen to my body :slight_smile: remember to do that." — TR forum

**Implication:** A serious, articulate segment rejects the premise that a number should drive the session. Summit's answer cannot be more insistence that the number is right. The defensible line is the one the product already holds — every recommendation traces to a rule and your own data, so you can *overrule it knowingly* — plus the fact that the free tier gives the interpretation without demanding you obey it. This is an objection to answer in the FAQ, not a segment to argue with.

## Theme 5 — Subscription fatigue · **Confidence: High**

- "Great tracking awful price… Will be moving to the Google fit air when the subscription ends. Once they lose me I won't be back." — WHOOP, 4★
- "$72.56 for a replacement band: Robbery." — WHOOP, 4★
- "The price is steep at $30/mo…" — TR forum, on Whoop
- "Overall though I wonder if this app is overpriced" — TrainerRoad, 4★
- "Free is alright; paid is not worth it." — TrainingPeaks, 2★

**Implication:** The strongest supporting evidence for the free-tier wedge. The home page's "what other apps charge for every month — with the watch you already own" is aimed at a real, repeatedly voiced grievance. Worth making more prominent, not less.

## Theme 6 — One app instead of five · **Confidence: Low-Medium**

Single platform, but the phrasing is strikingly close to Summit's own thesis, which makes it useful as validation *and* as a warning that it's not yet independently confirmed.

- "I would like one app that tracks across a few devices being a watch/wearable/bike computer, scale, and trainer. It then can mesh all the info together to prepare an adaptive plan." — TR forum
- Data-transfer friction recurs: "it's difficult to get your workout data uploaded… I can only upload it on a desktop computer and NOT using the app" — TrainingPeaks, 3★

## Vocabulary observed (their words, not ours)

**Used naturally:** readiness · recovery score · sleep score · body battery · HRV · resting HR · form / fitness / fatigue · CTL / ATL / TSB · TSS · FTP · ramp test · sweet spot · Z2 · progression levels · rest day · recovery week · "feel" · "listen to your body" · "junk miles" · structured workout · plan builder · head unit.

**Emotionally loaded phrases worth reusing:** "ruin your day" · "bear no resemblance to how I feel" · "lost all confidence in the system" · "logged as red as missed workouts" · "doesn't adapt to you" · "not what AI thinks I need to do" · "half baked" · "descriptive, not prescriptive" · "data everywhere and clarity nowhere" (ours, but it matches).

**Avoid:** "AI" in any product-facing claim; "optimize" as a bare verb; anything that implies the score overrules the athlete.

## Gaps — what this research still does not tell us

1. **No Summit users.** Everything is proxy. Zero evidence about what Summit specifically does well or badly.
2. **No Spanish or Catalan language data**, despite es/ca being two of three site locales.
3. **Reddit was not reachable** — its public JSON endpoint returns 403 without authentication, so r/Velo and r/cycling are absent from this pass. The TrainerRoad forum partly covers the same audience but skews to structured-training users.
4. **No pricing-sensitivity data** for Summit's actual tiers — the subscription-fatigue theme says people resent paying, not what they would pay.
5. **No trigger-event data.** Nothing here shows what makes someone go looking for a new training app on a given day.

**Suggested next passes:** authenticated Reddit access for r/Velo / r/cycling; Spanish-language sources (Foromtb, ciclismo subforums); and first-party review mining as soon as Summit accumulates App Store reviews.
