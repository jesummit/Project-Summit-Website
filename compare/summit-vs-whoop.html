Summit vs Whoop: a recovery score only counts if something changes

Whoop measures your body better than almost anything you can buy. The question for a cyclist is what happens to Thursday's intervals once it has.

By Jordi Espanyol · 5 Aug 2026 · 7 min read · Whoop facts checked 5 Aug 2026

Start with what Whoop does well, because it is a lot.

Whoop has spent years on a single problem — measuring a body continuously and turning that into one honest number every morning — and it is very good at it. The strap has no screen and nothing to look at, which makes it a smaller thing to sleep in than most watches; 24/7 wear is the design, not a compromise. Its sleep measurement has been checked against polysomnography, the clinical reference, in published work (Miller et al., Journal of Sports Sciences, 2020). If what you want is a continuous, well-studied read of your physiology, Whoop is a serious answer and nothing below is going to pretend otherwise.

Summit is not trying to be a better strap. It is not a strap at all. It is an iPhone app, and the thing it argues about is what happens after the number exists.

In short

Whoop is an instrument; Summit is a decision. Whoop gives you an excellent daily recovery figure on hardware it supplies and leaves the training call to you; Summit reads the wearable you already own through Apple Health and turns that same physiology into today's cycling session — with the recovery score and the sleep analysis free, permanently.

A recovery score is only worth what it changes

This is the whole argument, so it goes first.

Whoop scores your recovery superbly and then hands the number back to you. Its training-side output is a strain target: how hard today should be, on one scale that covers every activity you might do. That is a genuinely useful thing to know, and it is where Whoop stops. Nothing downstream of that number moves a cycling training plan, because Whoop is not holding one — it does not know that Thursday is 4×8 at threshold, that Saturday is the long ride the block is built around, or that your event is nine weeks out.

Summit is holding exactly that, which is why the number can act. A strong morning and today's session stands, or grows. An average one and it is left exactly as planned — quietly downgrading sessions every time a number dips is how riders lose fitness while feeling responsible. A genuinely bad morning and the hard work comes out but the hours on the bike stay, because on a rough day it is the intensity that costs you. Report an illness or an injury and the day stops entirely, and the key session you lost is rescheduled rather than quietly disappearing.

None of that is a chatbot and none of it is AI. The engine is deterministic: every call traces back to a codified sports-science rule applied to your own data, so you can see why it decided what it decided. That is the part Summit charges for — the plan that rearranges itself is Premium. The reading of your body is not.

Summit reads the wearable already on your wrist

Summit takes heart-rate variability (RMSSD), resting heart rate and sleep from Apple Health. Whatever wrote them there is Summit's sensor: an Apple Watch, a Garmin, an Oura ring, or something else that writes to Health — though not all of them write the same metrics, which is the next paragraph. There is no second device to buy, no second subscription, and nothing on the other wrist.

Worth being precise about two things, because both get assumed. First: there is no Summit watch app. Nothing to install on the watch, no complication, no on-wrist screen. The watch measures; the iPhone reads. Second: an Apple Watch is not required — but it is the only device that writes the full set Summit uses, which beyond HRV, resting heart rate and sleep also takes in respiratory rate, blood oxygen and overnight wrist temperature. Another wearable gives you a recovery score from what it does write; the Apple Watch gives the complete picture.

Whoop's model is the opposite by design, and deliberately so: one device, one sensor set, worn continuously, with nothing to configure and no permissions to get wrong. That is a real advantage, and it has its own section below.

In Summit, recovery and sleep are free — permanently, and that is not a trial

Four things cost nothing in Summit and are not scheduled to start costing anything:

  • the daily recovery score;
  • the sleep score with its full breakdown;
  • the recovery trend over time;
  • the Home and Lock Screen widgets.

That is a free tier, not a free trial. There is no counter running out and no card to enter. Premium — training load, power analysis, adaptive plans, workout export, race-day nutrition — is a separate thing, and Premium is what has the 14-day trial. Keeping those two apart matters: a "free" that quietly expires is a different product from one that does not.

What each one costs

Whoop membership, as listed on 5 August 2026: WHOOP One $199/year, WHOOP Peak $239/year, WHOOP Life $359/year. Every tier includes the device. Prices vary by country and do change — whoop.com/us/en/membership/ is the authority, not this page.

Which is why "Summit is cheaper" would be a dishonest sentence. That membership buys hardware; Summit does not sell you any. The fair comparison is this: if you already wear something that writes to Apple Health, Summit's recovery and sleep layer costs nothing on top of it. If you own nothing, you need a wearable either way — and Whoop bundling a good one into a single recurring price is a perfectly reasonable way to solve that.

Summit publishes no prices of its own. Apple sets Premium per storefront, so the App Store listing is the only source that is right in your country. What is free is on the plans page.

Cycling has its own numbers, and Summit is built on them

Whoop's Strain is one scale for everything you do, on purpose — that is what lets it put a hard ride, a lifting session and a day on skis on the same axis. For someone whose week is all three, that is more useful than a cycling model would be.

If your week is mostly riding, though, the sport has its own vocabulary and you eventually need it. Summit speaks it: chronic and acute training load and the form balance between them (CTL, ATL, TSB), eFTP, critical power, your power curve, and per-activity analysis of what a session actually was rather than how hard it felt. That is what tells you whether you are peaking on the right weekend or arriving flat — and it is Premium.

It also has to leave the app. Summit reads rides from Strava and Hammerhead Karoo, and exports workouts to Karoo and — via intervals.icu — to Garmin, Zwift and MyWhoosh, so the session the engine wrote is on the head unit on Saturday morning. Export is Premium too. Race-day fuelling is in the app; day-to-day nutrition that follows your training is on the roadmap, not shipped.

Where Whoop wins

Not a token section. These are the reasons to buy Whoop instead, and some of them are decisive.

One device, one dataset

Whoop owns the whole chain: the sensor, the sampling, the model. Nothing to permit, nothing to sync, no gap that turns out to be a Health setting you switched off six months ago. When a number is missing there is exactly one place to look. Summit's read is only ever as complete as what your wearable actually writes to Apple Health.

It is easier to sleep in

A fabric band with no screen is less watch than a watch. Plenty of people who will not wear a watch to bed will wear that, and a recovery score you actually collect every night beats a better one you collect four nights a week.

Android

Whoop runs on iOS and Android. Summit is iPhone only, iOS 26 or later, and there is no web version and no Android version planned to announce. If you carry an Android phone, this comparison is already over and the answer is Whoop.

One scale for a mixed week

If you ride, lift, run and ski, a sport-agnostic strain figure covering all of it is genuinely more useful than a cycling load model that only sees part of your week.

You never think about hardware

One recurring price, device included, replaced when it needs replacing. No deciding which wearable to buy, no working out whether it writes the right metrics to Health.

And they are not exclusive

Whoop can share data with Apple Health. If heart-rate variability, resting heart rate and sleep reach Health on your iPhone, Summit will read them from a Whoop strap the same way it reads them from anything else. Keeping both is a legitimate setup: Whoop as the instrument, Summit as the thing that turns it into Thursday.

See what your own numbers say on Friday

Install Summit, allow it to read your Health data, and wear whatever you already wear tonight. Three mornings from now you will have a recovery score, a full read of your sleep, and an answer to what today's ride should be. Free, permanently, with no trial to expire — deterministic sports-science maths running on your phone.

Questions people actually ask

Is Summit a Whoop alternative?

Partly, and it depends on what you want replaced. If you want recovery and sleep read every morning and then turned into a cycling plan, Summit does that and Whoop does not — provided you already wear something that writes to Apple Health. If what you want is the measuring device itself, Summit does not sell one. Whoop does, and its membership includes it.

Can I use Summit with a Whoop strap?

Summit reads heart-rate variability, resting heart rate and sleep from Apple Health, whatever wrote them there. Whoop can share data with Apple Health, so if those metrics reach Health on your iPhone, Summit will read them like any other source. Check what your Whoop app is set to write before assuming it — and note that only the Apple Watch writes the complete set Summit uses, which also takes in respiratory rate, blood oxygen and overnight wrist temperature.

Is Summit's recovery score really free, or is it a trial?

Free, permanently. The daily recovery score, the sleep score with its full breakdown, the recovery trend and the Home and Lock Screen widgets have no gate on them and no counter running out. Premium is a separate thing — training load, power analysis, adaptive plans, workout export and race-day nutrition — and Premium is what has a 14-day trial.

Do I need an Apple Watch to use Summit?

No. Any wearable that writes heart-rate variability, resting heart rate and sleep to Apple Health works, including Garmin and Oura — though only the Apple Watch writes the complete set Summit uses, which also takes in respiratory rate, blood oxygen and overnight wrist temperature, so anything else gives you a score from a partial picture. There is no Summit watch app: the watch is the sensor and the iPhone does the reading. Summit is iPhone only and needs iOS 26 or later.

How much does Whoop cost in 2026?

As listed on 5 August 2026, Whoop membership runs at $199/year for WHOOP One, $239 for WHOOP Peak and $359 for WHOOP Life, and every tier includes the device. Prices vary by country and change, so whoop.com/us/en/membership/ is the authority. Summit publishes no prices of its own: Apple sets Premium pricing per storefront, so the App Store listing is the only honest source.
