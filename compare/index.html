How these are written

A comparison page written by the people selling one of the two products is worth exactly as much as its unflattering parts. So:

We concede first

Every comparison opens with what the other product does better and carries a section devoted to it. If that section can't be written honestly, the page doesn't ship.

Facts carry a date

Every claim about another product says when we checked it and links to the authority — usually that company's own page. Products change; an undated comparison is one you can't trust.

No invented limitations

If we aren't confident another tool lacks something, we leave it out rather than imply it. The easiest way to write a comparison is to make the other side smaller than it is.

No prices for Summit

Apple sets Premium pricing per storefront, so any figure here would be wrong somewhere. The App Store listing is the only honest source; what's free is on the plans page.

No stars, no counts

No ratings, no user numbers, no testimonials, no "studies show" without a citation you can go and read. Same rule as the rest of this site.

Written, not generated

These are argued by a person who rides and built the app. Summit itself is not an AI product either — the engine is deterministic, and every call traces back to a rule and your own data.
