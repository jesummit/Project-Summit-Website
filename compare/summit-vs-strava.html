Summit vs Strava: one records the ride, the other decides it

Strava is where the ride counts and where your history already lives. Summit reads it, and answers a question Strava never set out to answer: what should today be?

By Jordi Espanyol · 5 Aug 2026 · 7 min read · Strava facts checked 5 Aug 2026

This is the one comparison on this site that should not end with you switching anything.

Strava is where the ride counts. Not in a training sense — in the sense that until it is on Strava it did not quite happen. It is where your club is, where the local Sunday hierarchy is settled on a two-kilometre climb, where you find out that the road you have never taken is the one everybody else takes. And it is where almost every cyclist's history already lives: years of rides, in one place, that nobody is going to move.

It is also not a training system, and it has never claimed to be one. Strava records and it connects. That is an enormous, well-executed job, and it is a different job from deciding what you should do on Thursday.

Summit does that second thing, and it does it on top of Strava rather than instead of it. Connect the two and your rides arrive in Summit on their own. Nothing is exported, nothing is re-uploaded, nothing leaves Strava.

In short

These two are complementary, not competing. Strava is where the ride is recorded, kept and shared; Summit reads those rides, reads your recovery from Apple Health, and turns both into what today's session should be. Nobody should leave Strava for Summit — Summit imports from it. The honest question is not "which one wins", it is "what is each one for".

Summit reads Strava — that is the relationship

Connect Strava once and rides land in Summit by themselves. Distance, duration, heart rate, power if you record it — the ride you already uploaded becomes the input to what happens next. There is nothing to export and no second upload, and your Strava account is unchanged: same kudos, same segments, same feed.

On the other side, Summit reads heart-rate variability, resting heart rate and sleep from Apple Health. Whatever wrote them there is the sensor: an Apple Watch, a Garmin, an Oura ring, or anything else that writes to Health. Worth being precise about one thing, because it gets assumed: there is no Summit watch app. Nothing to install on the watch, no complication, no on-wrist screen. The watch measures; the iPhone reads. And an Apple Watch is not required — any wearable that writes those metrics works.

So Strava supplies what you did, Apple Health supplies how you are, and Summit is the thing that has an opinion about tomorrow.

The question Strava was never built to answer

Open Strava after a ride and you get a complete, well-presented record of what just happened, plus everyone else's. What you do not get — because it is not what Strava is for — is a plan that changes.

That is the gap Summit fills. It holds the actual plan: that Thursday is 4×8 at threshold, that Saturday is the long ride the block is built around, that the event is nine weeks out. Which is what lets your morning numbers act. A strong morning and today's session stands, or grows. An average one and it is left exactly as planned — quietly downgrading sessions every time a number dips is how riders lose fitness while feeling responsible. A genuinely bad morning and the hard work comes out but the hours on the bike stay, because on a rough day it is the intensity that costs you. Tell it you only have ninety minutes on Saturday and the week rebuilds around ninety minutes. Report an illness or an injury and the day stops entirely, with the key session rescheduled rather than quietly disappearing.

None of that is AI and none of it is a chatbot. The engine is deterministic: every call traces back to a codified sports-science rule applied to your own data, so you can see why it decided what it decided. That is also the part Summit charges for — the plan that rearranges itself is Premium, along with training load (CTL, ATL, TSB), power analysis (eFTP, critical power, your power curve), per-activity detailed analysis, workout export and race-day fuelling. Day-to-day nutrition that follows your training is on the roadmap, not shipped.

What is free in Summit — permanently, and not a trial

Four things cost nothing and are not scheduled to start costing anything:

  • the daily recovery score;
  • the sleep score with its full breakdown;
  • the recovery trend over time;
  • the Home and Lock Screen widgets.

That is a free tier, not a free trial. There is no counter running out and no card to enter. Premium is a separate thing, and Premium is what has the 14-day trial. Keeping those two apart matters: a "free" that quietly expires is a different product from one that does not.

What each one costs

Strava, as listed on 5 August 2026: a standard subscription in the United States is $11.99/month or $79.99/year plus applicable taxes, with a Family plan at $139.99/year and a Strava + Runna plan at $149.99/year. Pricing varies by region and changes — strava.com/pricing is the authority, not this page.

What sits behind that subscription and what does not is something this page deliberately will not tell you. That line has moved more than once, and a stale answer here would be worse than none. What Strava's own help centre does say is that when a subscription expires you "will only lose access to the paid features temporarily" (Subscription Pricing FAQ, checked 5 August 2026) — an unsubscribed account keeps working, and plenty of riders have never needed more than that.

Summit publishes no prices of its own. Apple sets Premium per storefront, so the App Store listing is the only source that is right in your country. What is free is on the plans page.

Where Strava wins

Not a token section. On everything below, Strava is not merely better than Summit — Summit does not compete at all.

The community and the feed

Kudos, comments, clubs, the small social pressure of people seeing that you went out in the rain. That is a genuine training aid for a lot of riders and Summit has none of it, on purpose. A training app that also wanted your attention every evening would be a worse training app.

Segments and local competition

Segment leaderboards, filtered by follower, club, age and weight, turn every ordinary road into something with a scoreboard (Strava's own feature list, checked 5 August 2026). Nothing in Summit replaces that, and nothing in Summit tries.

Route discovery and heatmaps

Route creation, Suggested Routes and personal heatmaps — Strava describes the last as "unique visualizations of the ground you've covered worldwide" (same page, checked 5 August 2026). Finding good roads in an unfamiliar place is a solved problem there, and an unsolved one everywhere else.

Multi-sport breadth

Strava groups its activity types into five categories — foot sports, cycle sports, water sports, winter sports and everything else — covering dozens of activities (supported sport types, checked 5 August 2026). Summit is cycling only, deliberately. If your week includes a run, a swim and a ski day, one of these two is holding all of it and it is not Summit.

It runs everywhere

Strava ships an iOS app and an Android app (strava.com/mobile) and has a full website. Summit is iPhone only, iOS 26 or later, with no Android version and no web version to announce. If you do not carry an iPhone, Summit is not available to you and Strava is.

Your history is already there

Years of rides, with the photos and the comments attached. Nobody should be asked to walk away from that, and Summit's answer is not to ask: it reads from Strava precisely so that the history stays where it is.

Keep Strava. Add the part it was never for.

Install Summit, connect Strava, and allow it to read your Health data. Your rides arrive on their own, and within a few mornings you have a recovery score, a full read of your sleep, and an answer to what today should be. Free, permanently, with no trial to expire — deterministic sports-science maths running on your phone.

Questions people actually ask

Should I stop using Strava if I install Summit?

No, and please do not. Summit reads your rides from Strava, so leaving Strava would take away the thing Summit runs on. They do different jobs: Strava records the ride and connects you to the people who ride near you; Summit decides what today's session should be. Keeping both is the intended setup, not a compromise.

Does Summit import my Strava rides automatically?

Yes. Connect Strava once and rides arrive on their own; you do not export or upload anything. Summit also reads rides from Hammerhead Karoo, and exports workouts to Karoo and, via intervals.icu, to Garmin, Zwift and MyWhoosh.

Does Summit have segments, kudos or a feed?

No. There is no feed, no kudos, no clubs, no segments and no leaderboards in Summit, and none are planned. That layer is Strava's and Strava is very good at it. Summit is a training app you open in the morning to find out what to do, not a place to be seen doing it.

Pricing varies by region and changes, so strava.com/pricing is the authority. Summit publishes no prices of its own: Apple sets Premium pricing per storefront, so the App Store listing is the only honest source.

Is Summit's recovery score really free, or is it a trial?

Free, permanently. The daily recovery score, the sleep score with its full breakdown, the recovery trend and the Home and Lock Screen widgets have no gate on them and no counter running out. Premium is a separate thing, and Premium is what has a 14-day trial.
